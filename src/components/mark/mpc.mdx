# Multi-Party Computation
Multi-Party Computation (MPC) is a family of protocols whose aim is to let a collection of $n$ parties
carry out some computation. Namely each party has some input $w_i$ which they would like to keep
private, and have knowledge of public information $x$. Together they wish to compute
some **functionality**
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$f(w_1,\dots,w_n, x)$$
</div>
without revealing their private inputs.

These protocols consist of (possibly many) rounds of private computation, and communication between
the parties, with the final result being the computation of the value of $f(w_1,\dots,w_n, x)$

For the purpose of working towards MPC in the Head, we often consider the private inputs $w_i$ to be
shares of some secret (or a collection of shares of several secrets). 
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$w_i=([a]_i,[b]_i,\dots)$$
</div>
Lets take the example of MPC 
protocols which work upon additive secret shares.
- Other MPC protocols exist which work for other secret sharing schemes, for example 
Ben-Or-Goldwasser-Widgerson (BGW) protocols are certain MPC protocols designed around Shamir secret
shares.

Also for simplicity lets assume the functionality $f$ can be broken down into several basic arithmetic
operations.
## Linear operations
### Addition of secrets and public values
Given two secrets $a,b$ with a collection of shares $[a]_i,[b]_i$ which each party holds, notice that
if each party can compute a valid secret sharing of the sum $a+b$ by adding the individual shares
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$[a+b]_i=[a]_i+[b]_i$$
</div>
It is not hard to see that indeed
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$\sum_i[a+b]_i=\sum_i[a]_i+\sum_i[b]_i=a+b$$
</div>

Now to add a public constant $x_0$ to a secret $a$, one can either choose one party (say the first)
to add $x_0$ to their share, or have all $n$ parties add $x_0\cdot n^{-1}$ to their share (where 
$n^{-1}$ is the appropriate multiplicative inverse in $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$).
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$[a+x_0]_i=[a]_i+x_0\cdot n^{-1}$$
</div>
Both these operations require no communication rounds.

### Scaling by public values
Multiplication of two secrets from their shares is typically a lot more difficult, however 
multiplication of a secret $a$ by a public value $x_0$ just involves multiplying every
share by $x_0$:
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$[x_0\cdot a]_i=x_0\cdot [a]_i$$
</div>

## Multiplication of secret values with Replicated Secret Sharing
{/* Mention something on beaver triples as an alternative */}
The difficulty of multiplication in additive secret sharing comes from the need to calculate
all the products of the form $[a]_i[b]_j$:
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$a\cdot b=\sum_{i,j}[a]_i[b]_j$$
</div>
There are several subprotocols to carry out the multiplication of two secrets from their
shares. We will take a look at an example called **replicated secret sharing**.
<br />
A naive but evidently insecure version of this plays out as so; say we have five parties, which
we colour code <span style={{ color:'red' }}>Alice (1)</span>,
<span style={{ color:'orange' }}>Bob (2)</span>,
<span style={{ color:'green' }}>Cole (3)</span>,
<span style={{ color:'blue' }}>Dahlia (4)</span>,
<span style={{ color:'purple' }}>Ellie (5)</span>,
then each is tasked to compute the following products of their shares in this following manner:
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$\begin{matrix} \red{[a]_1[b]_1} & \red{[a]_1[b]_2} & \red{[a]_1[b]_3} & \blue{[a]_1[b]_4} & \purple{[a]_1[b]_5} \\ \red{[a]_2[b]_1} & \orange{[a]_2[b]_2} & \orange{[a]_2[b]_3} & \orange{[a]_2[b]_4} & \purple{[a]_2[b]_5} \\ \red{[a]_3[b]_1} & \orange{[a]_3[b]_2} & \green{[a]_3[b]_3} & \green{[a]_3[b]_4} & \green{[a]_3[b]_5} \\ \blue{[a]_4[b]_1} & \orange{[a]_4[b]_2} & \green{[a]_4[b]_3} & \blue{[a]_4[b]_4} & \blue{[a]_4[b]_5} \\ \purple{[a]_5[b]_1} & \purple{[a]_5[b]_2} & \green{[a]_5[b]_3} & \blue{[a]_5[b]_4} & \purple{[a]_5[b]_5} \\ \end{matrix}$$
</div>
- Each party sends their share to $\text{ceil}(n/2)$ other parties (this step is obviously insecre!). 
For example, Dahlia sends her shares to Ellie and Alice.
- Each party then computes their products, namely Alice recieves $\red{[a]_2,[b]_2,[a]_3,[b]_3}$. from Bob 
and Cole, and calculates
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$\red{[a\cdot b]_1=[a]_1[b]_1+[a]_2[b]_1+[a]_3[b]_1+[a]_1[b]_2+[a]_1[b]_3}.$$
</div>
Together, all parties would now have computed all the share products $[a]_i[b]_j$, and so their sum
is equal to $a\cdot b$:
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$a\cdot b=\sum_i [a\cdot b]_i.$$
</div>
Again for the goal of maintaining privacy of the other initial inputs, the original shares should not be 
known by other parties. It is best if we have a way to randomly create a new set of shares which can be
messaged to other people such that the original share is not known.

### Key/secret resharing
To create a new set of shares to the original, Each party performs their own additive secret sharing on 
their share to split it into $n$ smaller shares:
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$[a]_i=\sum_j [a]_{i,j}.$$
</div>
Each party sends the $j$th share to the $j$th party only, then each party takes their new set of shares
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$[a]'_j=\sum_i [a]_{i,j}.$$
</div>
Evidently these new shares $[a]'_j$ all still add up to the original $a$
<div style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
$$\sum_j[a]'_j=\sum_j\sum_i [a]_{i,j}=\sum_i[a]_i=a.$$
</div>
The parties are now safe to message these new shares without revealing information about the old shares.
