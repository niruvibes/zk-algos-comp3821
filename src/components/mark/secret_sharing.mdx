# Secret Sharing Schemes
**Secret sharing** refers to different schemes to distribute some secret $s$ among a group of $n$ parties, 
such that only particular subset of parties can reconstruct the secret. Namely a set of **shares** $[s]_1,\dots,[s]_n$
is handed out to the parties, and given knowledge of enough of them, one can determine what $s$ is. 

The most common type of sharing scheme is one where any group of more than $t$ party shares can reconstruct the secret $s$, 
but any group $t$ shares or less cannot determine any information on the secret. These are captured within the
properties:
- $t$**-reconstructability** which says the algorithm for secret reconstruction is guaranteed to produce correct results given more than $t$ shares.
- $t$**-privacy** which says that given $t$ or less shares, one cannot learn any information about the secret $s$.

## Additive Secret Sharing
A simple but effective example is **additive secret sharing**. For a given secret input number in some large prime
order field $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ (that is in the field of arithmetic modulo some prime $p$), we can randomly pick
$n$ shares which add up to the secret.
Namely our sharing algorithm involves picking $n-1$ share numbers $[s]_1,\dots,[s]_{n-1}$ uniformly distributed 
across $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$, and producing the last share by $[s]_n=s-[s]_1-\cdots-[s]_{n-1}$.
